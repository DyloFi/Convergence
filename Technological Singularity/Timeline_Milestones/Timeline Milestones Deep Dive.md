## 1. Historical & Contemporary Milestone Chronology (1950–2026)

### 1.1 The Theoretical & Symbolic Era (1950–2011) for Empirical Milestone Timeline & FLOP Roadmap (1950–2050)
- **1950**: Alan Turing publishes *Computing Machinery and Intelligence*, introducing operational criteria for machine cognition (Turing, 1950).
- **1956**: Dartmouth Summer Research Project establishes AI as a formal scientific discipline.
- **1965**: I.J. Good formulates the "Intelligence Explosion" concept (Good, 1965).
- **1986**: Rumelhart, Hinton, and Williams publish backpropagation for multi-layer neural networks (Rumelhart et al., 1986).
- **1993**: Vernor Vinge publishes *The Coming Technological Singularity*, defining the event horizon metaphor (Vinge, 1993).
- **1997**: IBM Deep Blue defeats world chess champion Garry Kasparov ($10^{11}$ FLOPs/sec custom ASIC execution).
- **2005**: Ray Kurzweil publishes *The Singularity Is Near*, formalizing the Law of Accelerating Returns (Kurzweil, 2005).

### 1.2 The Deep Learning Scaling Era (2012–2023) for Empirical Milestone Timeline & FLOP Roadmap (1950–2050)
- **2012 (AlexNet Milestone)**: Deep convolutional neural networks trained on GPUs win ImageNet, igniting the deep learning boom ($\sim 1.4 \times 10^{17}$ FLOPs) (Sevilla et al., 2022).
- **2014**: Nick Bostrom publishes *Superintelligence*, formalizing Orthogonality and Instrumental Convergence (Bostrom, 2014).
- **2016**: DeepMind AlphaGo defeats world champion Lee Sedol, combining deep neural networks with Monte Carlo Tree Search.
- **2017**: Vaswani et al. publish *Attention Is All You Need* (Transformer architecture) ($\sim 10^{21}$ FLOPs).
- **2020**: OpenAI releases GPT-3 (175B parameters, $3.14 \times 10^{23}$ FLOPs), establishing empirical scaling laws (Kaplan et al., 2020).
- **2023**: Release of GPT-4 and Gemini 1.0 ($\sim 10^{25}$ FLOPs), demonstrating human-level performance on professional bar and medical licensing exams.

### 1.3 The Frontier & Test-Time Search Era (2024–2026) for Empirical Milestone Timeline & FLOP Roadmap (1950–2050)
- **2024**: Breakthrough in System 2 Test-Time Compute Scaling, utilizing Process Reward Models (PRMs) and tree search during inference.
- **2025**: SWE-bench Verified performance passes 70%, enabling automated software bug resolution across major repositories.
- **2026**: Frontier training runs execute at $10^{26}$ FLOP scale on 100,000+ chip GPU/TPU clusters (NVIDIA Blackwell B200), triggering mandatory sovereign notification thresholds.

### Mathematical Transition Function of Paradigm Acceleration

The compression of historical milestone durations $\Delta t_n = t_{n} - t_{n-1}$ across consecutive technological paradigms $n \in \{1, 2, \dots, N\}$ follows an inverse power scaling relation:

$$\Delta t_n = \Delta t_0 \cdot \lambda^{-n}$$

where $\lambda \approx 2.41$ represents the empirically estimated acceleration constant. The cumulative time to the theoretical inflection boundary converges to a finite-horizon singularity:

$$T_{\text{inflection}} = t_0 + \Delta t_0 \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \lambda^{-n} = t_0 + \frac{\Delta t_0}{\lambda - 1} < \infty$$

### Phase Space Compression Function

The transition interval $\Delta T_n$ between the $(n)$th and $(n+1)$th technological paradigm scales as:
$$\Delta T_n = \Delta T_0 \cdot \beta^{-n}, \quad \text{where } \beta > 1.25$$
This recursive compression produces a geometric convergence point $T_{\infty} = T_0 + \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \Delta T_n = T_0 + \frac{\Delta T_0}{1 - \beta^{-1}}$, validating the theoretical asymptote of the Law of Accelerating Returns when physical energy and communication bandwidth permit continuous paradigm turnover.
