## 1. Theoretical Framework & Conceptual Taxonomy

The theoretical landscape of long-wave economics is structured across four primary sub-disciplines:

### 1.1 Kondratiev Capital Accumulation & Depreciation Dynamics for Concept Directory Guide & Structural Taxonomy
Nikolai Kondratiev (1925, 1935) demonstrated that basic physical capital infrastructure (canals, railways, power grids, highway networks, telecommunication backbones) requires massive initial capital expenditures that depreciate over 40- to 60-year horizons. The replacement and renewal of this basic infrastructure generates long cycles of economic expansion (A-Phase) and contraction (B-Phase).

### 1.2 Schumpeterian Innovation Swarms & Creative Destruction for Concept Directory Guide & Structural Taxonomy
Joseph Schumpeter (1939) integrated technological innovation into Kondratiev's framework, demonstrating that long waves are driven by **clusters of radical general-purpose technologies (GPTs)**. Entrepreneurial risk-taking lowers adoption barriers for followers, creating an innovation "swarm" that propels the A-phase expansion.

### 1.3 Perez Socio-Technical Paradigms for Concept Directory Guide & Structural Taxonomy
Carlota Perez (2002) formulated the co-evolution of **Financial Capital** and **Production Capital**:
- **Installation Phase (Concept Directory Guide & Structural Taxonomy)**: Irruption and Frenzy periods, where financial capital funds technological infrastructure, culminating in major financial bubbles.
- **Turning Point Crisis (Concept Directory Guide & Structural Taxonomy)**: Financial collapses (e.g., 1847, 1893, 1929, 2000, 2008) forcing state regulation and institutional recalibration.
- **Deployment Phase (Concept Directory Guide & Structural Taxonomy)**: Synergy and Maturity periods, where production capital generates broad productivity expansion.

## 3. Comparative Perspectives, Critiques & Counter-Theories (README)

Conceptual long-wave frameworks must be continually contextualized against major econometric critiques:

1. **Spectral Analysis & Filter Artifacts (Solomou, 1987; Kuznets, 1930)**: Econometricians show that trend-elimination filters (moving averages, HP filters) applied to macro time series artificially produce smooth 50-year waves—the **Slutsky-Yule Effect**. Spectral density estimation on raw un-filtered GDP data fails to confirm 50-year periodicity.
2. **Stochastic Trend Critique (Nelson & Plosser, 1982)**: Empirical testing challenged deterministic wave models, asserting that random macroeconomic shocks account for persistent trend shifts without requiring underlying long-wave periodicity.
$$Y_{t, kondratiev_wave} = \mu_{kondratiev_wave} + Y_{t-1, kondratiev_wave} + \epsilon_{t, kondratiev_wave}$$
   Shocks produce permanent structural shifts rather than temporary deviations around a deterministic 50-year sine wave.
3. **Marxist Exogenous Shock Model (Trotsky, 1923; Mandel, 1975)**: Leon Trotsky argued that long-term macroeconomic shifts are non-periodic, driven by exogenous geopolitical shocks and war.
4. **Neoclassical Growth Theory (Solow, 1956)**: Solow's model treats technical change as smooth exogenous total factor productivity growth $A(t) = A_0 e^{gt}$.

### Integration Protocol for Econometric Evaluation

To guarantee empirical reproducibility across all Kondratiev Waves research dossiers, investigators apply standardized filtering and signal extraction routines:
1. **Data Pre-processing**: Transforming nominal historical time series into log-differenced real series adjusted using chained inflation deflators.
2. **Trend-Cycle Decomposition**: Implementing multi-stage Baxter-King and Hodrick-Prescott filters to eliminate high-frequency business cycles ($T < 8 \text{ years}$) and medium Kuznets swings ($T \in [15, 25] \text{ years}$).
3. **Spectral Density Estimation**: Computing Welch periodograms and continuous wavelet transforms to detect localized frequency drift across technological paradigm boundaries.
4. **Falsification Benchmarking**: Testing filtered cycles against synthetic non-stationary autoregressive ARIMA(1,1,0) null hypotheses using Monte Carlo simulations.
