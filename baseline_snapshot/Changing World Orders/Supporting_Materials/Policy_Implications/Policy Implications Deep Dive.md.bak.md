# Public Policy, State Governance, and Industrial Strategy in Changing World Orders

## Executive Summary — Policy Implications
This research monograph examines how sovereign governments adapt public policy, tax codes, trade regulations, national security mandates, and industrial subsidies during late-stage hegemonic transitions (Dalio 2021; Gilpin 1981). It analyzes the shift from late 20th-century neoliberal laissez-faire globalization to 2020s state-directed industrial strategy, contrasting Western state intervention frameworks (US CHIPS Act, EU Critical Raw Materials Act) with non-Western state-capitalism models (China's Dual Circulation Strategy, Made in China 2025) (Allison 2017; Keohane 1984).

### A. United States CHIPS and Science Act (2022) & Inflation Reduction Act
- **Strategic Imperative**: Re-shoring advanced logic wafer fabrication (TSMC Arizona, Intel Ohio, Samsung Texas) to eliminate single-point-of-failure vulnerabilities in the Taiwan Strait (Allison 2017).
- **Fiscal Commitment**: $52.7 billion in direct manufacturing subsidies, $24 billion in 25% advanced manufacturing tax credits, and $200 billion in scientific R&D authorization over 10 years.

### B. China's Dual Circulation Strategy & Made in China 2025
- **Strategic Imperative**: Insulate China's domestic economy against Western sanctions ("Internal Circulation") while expanding global market reliance on Chinese industrial exports ("External Circulation") (Dalio 2021).
- **Fiscal Commitment**: Channeled over $300 billion via state-owned banks and the National Integrated Circuit Industry Investment Fund (Big Fund Phases I–III) to achieve self-sufficiency across 10 critical high-tech sectors.

### C. European Union Critical Raw Materials & Chips Act
- **Strategic Imperative**: Mandate that by 2030 no more than 65% of any strategic raw material (lithium, rare earths, cobalt) be imported from a single third country.
- **Fiscal Commitment**: €43 billion public-private investment package to raise European semiconductor manufacturing share from 9% to 20% of global output by 2030.


## 2. Advanced Statecraft Tools & Administrative Mechanics (Policy Implications)

Late-stage hegemonic transitions require states to deploy economic statecraft tools to protect national security (Gilpin 1981):

### A. Inbound & Outbound Investment Screening (CFIUS & Executive Orders)
- **CFIUS Expansion**: Expanded jurisdiction under the Foreign Investment Risk Review Modernization Act (FIRRMA) to review foreign real estate acquisitions near military sites and critical technology investments.
- **Outbound Investment Controls**: Mandatory notification and prohibition of US venture capital/private equity funding for Chinese entities developing advanced AI, quantum computing, and sub-5nm semiconductors.

### B. Financial Sanctions & SWIFT Exclusion Architectures
- **Specially Designated Nationals (SDN) List**: Treasury Department Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) enforcement freezing USD assets and enforcing secondary sanctions against foreign institutions doing business with sanctioned entities.
- **SWIFT Disconnection**: Disconnecting adversary bank central clearing accounts to cut off global dollar settlement capability (Dalio 2021).

### C. Strategic Export Controls & Entity List Restrictions
- **Foreign Direct Product Rule (FDPR)**: Restricting foreign companies (e.g., ASML, TSMC) from selling chips or machinery containing >25% US-origin technology or software to entities listed on the US Commerce Entity List.


## 3. Comprehensive Policy Transformation Matrix (Policy Implications)

The table below contrasts policy postures between stable unipolar eras and late-stage transition epochs (Gilpin 1981; Keohane 1984):

| Public Policy Domain | Neoliberal Unipolar Era (1990–2015) | Hegemonic Transition Era (2026–2035) | Strategic Policy Rationale |
| :--- | :---- | :----- | :------ |
| **Trade Policy** | Tariffs <3%; Free Trade (GATT/WTO); Offshoring | Tariffs 10%–60%+; Near-shoring; Friend-shoring | Rebuild domestic supply chain autonomy |
| **Industrial Subsidies** | Minimal state aid; Anti-subsidy WTO enforcement | Massive subsidies ($50B+ packages); Tax credits | Secure critical technological assets |
| **Capital Mobility** | Unrestricted global FX and equity flows | Outbound/Inbound screening; Asset freezes | Prevent capital transfer to peer rivals |
| **Defense Procurement** | Peace Dividend (~3% of GDP); Anti-insurgency | High Spending (4%–5%+ GDP); Hypersonics/AI | Deter high-intensity Great Power collision |
| **Energy Governance** | Lowest-cost market integration | Strategic storage; Sovereign grid protection | Mitigate sea-lane blockade risks |


## 4. Policy Roadmap for Mid-Sized Sovereign States (2026–2035)

1. **Strategic Autonomy & Dual-Hedging**: Maintain non-aligned, transactional diplomatic relations, securing trade access to both G7 and BRICS+ markets (Allison 2017).
2. **Critical Infrastructure Hardening**: Mandate domestic ownership of national telecommunication backbones, power grids, and central bank digital clearing platforms.
3. **Sovereign Capital Reserving**: Diversify sovereign wealth fund allocations into physical gold bullion, strategic energy assets, and local-currency sovereign debt (Dalio 2021).
