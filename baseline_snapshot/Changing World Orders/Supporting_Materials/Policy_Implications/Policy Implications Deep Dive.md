# Public Policy, State Governance, and Industrial Strategy in Changing World Orders

## Executive Summary — Policy Implications
This research monograph examines how sovereign states adapt public policy, tax codes, trade regulations, national security mandates, and industrial subsidies during late-stage hegemonic transitions (Dalio 2021; Gilpin 1981). It analyzes the shift from late 20th-century neoliberal laissez-faire globalization to 2020s state-directed techno-industrial strategy, contrasting Western state intervention frameworks (US CHIPS Act, Inflation Reduction Act, EU Critical Raw Materials Act) with non-Western state-capitalism models (China'''s Dual Circulation Strategy, Made in China 2025) (Allison 2017; Keohane 1984; Rodrik 2011).

### 2.1 Export Controls and Technological Chokepoint Policy
The primary policy lever deployed by the United States is the unilateral weaponization of extraterritorial export control regulations through the Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) Entity List and Foreign Direct Product Rules (FDPR) (Farrell & Newman 2019; Allison 2017). By barring global foundries from utilizing US-origin EDA software, lithography equipment, and transistor patents to produce advanced GPUs for Chinese entities, US policy seeks to freeze China'''s computational frontier (Miller 2022).

### 2.2 Counter-Asymmetric State Capitalism
In response, the People'''s Republic of China deploys whole-of-nation mobilization (*juguo tizhi*): direct state equity investments through the National Integrated Circuit Industry Investment Fund (the "Big Fund"), preferential energy tariffs for domestic foundries, and aggressive market-share expansion in legacy mature-node semiconductors (28nm to 90nm) (Naughton 2007; Pettis 2020).

When interest expense on sovereign debt exceeds the national defense appropriation—as occurred in the United States in fiscal year 2024—fiscal constraints directly impair forward military procurement, forcing governments to choose between domestic entitlement cuts and military retrenchment (Ferguson 2001; Kennedy 1987).

## 5. Master Synthesis Policy Integration

The policy frameworks derived here link directly to the Master Synthesis:
- **Technological Subsidies as Defensive Sovereignty**: Industrial policy in the 2020s is not merely economic stimulus; it is the state'''s defensive intervention to preserve sovereign computation capacity in the face of supply chain weaponization (Miller 2022).
- **Macroeconomic Realignment**: When fiscal exhaustion constrains direct budgetary outlays, state policy shifts toward regulatory mandates, private-public consortia, and tariff walls, accelerating the morphological fragmentation of the global economy (Dalio 2021; Gilpin 1981).

## 6. Financial Sanctions Defensive Shielding and Reserve Architecture

Sovereign central banks outside the G7 coalition have initiated structural policy shifts to insulate balance sheets against extraterritorial asset freezes:
1. **Physical Gold Repatriation**: Non-Western central banks have accelerated gold purchases to over 1,000 metric tons annually, repatriating gold bars to domestic vaults to eliminate third-party jurisdiction risks (Arslanalp et al. 2022).
2. **Local Currency Clearing Bilateralism**: Bilateral local currency swaps (such as China-Russia, India-Russia, and China-Brazil agreements) bypass the New York CHIPS messaging and clearing infrastructure entirely, reducing transaction costs and eliminating OFAC sanction surface area (Subacchi 2016).
3. **Sovereign Wealth Relocation**: Reallocating sovereign wealth assets from foreign government bonds into physical domestic infrastructure, mineral concessions, and equity stakes across neutral jurisdictions (Dalio 2021).

## 7. Macroprudential Defense and Sovereign Liquidity Architecture

In late-phase hegemonic competition, sovereign monetary authorities must insulate domestic capital systems from exogenous dollar-clearing contractions (Dalio 2021; Pettis 2020):
- **Bilateral Currency Swap Networks**: Establishing extensive bilateral swap lines denominated in domestic accounting units (e.g., PBoC swap lines exceeding 4.1 trillion RMB across 40 jurisdictions) guarantees liquidity backstops independent of Federal Reserve swap facilities (Subacchi 2016).
- **Cross-Border Interbank Alternatives**: Integration of distributed ledger settlement platforms (such as the mBridge platform operated by the BIS Innovation Hub and central banks of China, Thailand, UAE, and Hong Kong) enables real-time peer-to-peer foreign exchange settlement without routing through US correspondent banks (Farrell & Newman 2019).
- **Collateral Re-anchoring Protocols**: Gradually transitioning sovereign collateral frameworks for domestic repo operations away from foreign sovereign debt obligations toward sovereign-held bullion, export commodities, and domestic infrastructure bonds (Reinhart & Rogoff 2009).
