## 3. Detailed Breakdown of Core Macro Theoretical Paradigms

### A. Robert Gilpin's Hegemonic War Theory for Comprehensive Theoretical Compendium of Macro-Geopolitical Cycles
- **Core Concept**: Systemic war occurs when the distribution of international power shifts faster than existing institutional rules and territorial boundaries can adapt (Gilpin 1981).
- **Mechanism**: An incumbent hegemon experiences rising costs of maintaining the international status quo, while a rising power gains economic capability to challenge the order at lower relative cost.

### B. Paul Kennedy's Imperial Overstretch Thesis for Comprehensive Theoretical Compendium of Macro-Geopolitical Cycles
- **Core Concept**: Empires fail when foreign military commitments expand beyond what the domestic economic and industrial base can support (Kennedy 1987).
- **Mechanism**: Diverting capital from domestic education, R&D, and infrastructure into foreign military garrisons erodes long-term competitiveness, making fiscal insolvency inevitable.

### C. Immanuel Wallerstein & Fernand Braudel's World-Systems Analysis for Comprehensive Theoretical Compendium of Macro-Geopolitical Cycles
- **Core Concept**: The capitalist world-economy is divided into Core, Semi-Periphery, and Periphery regions (Wallerstein 1974; Braudel 1984).
- **Financialization Autumn**: The shift from productive manufacturing to liquid financial asset speculation marks the autumn of hegemony, signalling capital migration to semi-peripheral production centers.

### D. Ray Dalio's Big Cycle Model for Comprehensive Theoretical Compendium of Macro-Geopolitical Cycles
- **Core Concept**: Evaluates imperial lifespans (~250 years) driven by the interaction of the Long-Term Debt Cycle, Internal Order/Disorder Cycle, and External Order Cycle (Dalio 2021).
