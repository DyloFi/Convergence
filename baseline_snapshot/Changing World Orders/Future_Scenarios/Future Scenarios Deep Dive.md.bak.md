# Future Scenarios for the Changing World Order (2026–2035): Quantitative & Geopolitical Modeling

## Executive Summary — Future Scenarios
This research monograph models three plausible global structural scenarios for the evolution of international power, global currency regimes, and institutional governance over the next decade (2026–2035). Grounded in geopolitical game theory, debt-sustainability modeling, Hegemonic Stability Theory (Kindleberger 1973; Gilpin 1981), and Ray Dalio's *Big Cycle* framework (Dalio 2021), it establishes quantitative stress-test parameters (inflation rates, sovereign yield spreads, FX volatility bounds, central bank debt monetization thresholds) for each scenario (Allison 2017).

## 2. Granular Scenario Analysis

### Scenario 1 — Managed Multipolar Alignment (Bretton Woods III)
- **Primary Triggers**: Soaring sovereign debt servicing costs in major Western economies (>20% of tax revenue) combined with catastrophic supply chain disruption costs lead to bilateral US-China "guardrail accords" (Dalio 2021).
- **Economic & Monetary Mechanics**: Transition from unipolar dollar dominance to a multi-asset reserve clearing architecture (Bretton Woods III). Sovereign balance sheets settle cross-border claims using a basket of Gold, US Dollars, Euros, Renminbi, and physical commodities managed via neutral central bank digital currency (CBDC) platforms like mBridge.
- **Geopolitical Stability**: Formal recognition of regional spheres of influence in East Asia and Eurasia paired with binding multilateral treaties governing artificial intelligence compute thresholds and space/cyber warfare (Ikenberry 2011).

### Scenario 2 — Fragmented Economic Cold War (Baseline Projection)
- **Primary Triggers**: Unrelenting escalation of US semiconductor export controls (restricting sub-5nm chips and HBM memory) and Chinese counter-embargoes on critical minerals (gallium, germanium, antimony, refined rare earths) (Allison 2017).
- **Economic & Monetary Mechanics**: Systemic bifurcation of global trade into two non-interoperable trade and currency ecosystems:
  1. *Western Bloc*: Anchored by the US Dollar, SWIFT, G7 near-shoring networks, and NATO/AUKUS defense pacts.
  2. *Eurasian / Global South Bloc*: Anchored by the Chinese Renminbi, CIPS, local currency bilateral trade, BRICS+ payment clearing, and Eurasian overland freight corridors (Mackinder 1904).
- **Geopolitical Stability**: Persistent high-intensity proxy conflicts in Eastern Europe, the Middle East, and Southeast Asia, accompanied by state-sponsored cyber disruption, without direct kinetic nuclear collision (Mearsheimer 2001).

### Scenario 3 — Systemic Monetary Crisis & Kinetic Collision
- **Primary Triggers**: Tactical miscalculation in the Taiwan Strait or a Red Sea maritime blockade resulting in kinetic engagement between carrier strike groups and land-based anti-ship missile batteries (Mahan 1890).
- **Economic & Monetary Mechanics**: Triggering of catastrophic sanctions, immediate SWIFT exclusion of Chinese financial institutions, freezing of $1.5T in foreign-held assets, and instant supply chain seizure. Western central banks respond with emergency Yield Curve Control (YCC) and massive debt monetization, driving hyper-inflationary currency debasement and flight to physical gold and hard commodities (Kindleberger 1973; Dalio 2021).

## 4. Strategic Recommendations for Decision Makers (2026–2035)

- **Macro Portfolio Investors**: Maintain 20%–30% capital allocation to hard physical assets (gold bullion, copper, energy infrastructure, agricultural land) to hedge against Scenario 2 baseline inflation and Scenario 3 monetization shocks (Dalio 2021).
- **Corporate Supply Chain Officers**: Re-shore or near-shore critical inputs to eliminate single-point-of-failure vulnerabilities in high-risk maritime chokepoints (Taiwan Strait, Malacca).
- **Sovereign Policymakers**: Expand national strategic petroleum and critical mineral reserves, while developing multi-currency bilateral settlement mechanisms to protect national trade against SWIFT disconnection risks (Allison 2017).
