## 1. Core Academic Research Questions

The research plan is organized around five central analytical inquiries:

1. **Monetary & Reserve Currency Lifecycles**: By what quantitative thresholds and lead-lag intervals does reserve currency dominance decline relative to underlying manufacturing productivity and global trade share (Dalio 2021; Braudel 1984)?
2. **Structural Power Transition & Conflict Risk**: Under what empirical conditions does a rising power's growth trajectory trigger kinetic war versus managed institutional realignment (Allison 2017; Organski & Kugler 1980)?
3. **Internal Disorder Mechanics**: How do domestic wealth polarization (Gini coefficient), fiscal deficit monetization, and central bank balance sheet expansion interact to destabilize internal governance structures (Dalio 2021; Kennedy 1987)?
4. **Geopolitical Landpower vs. Seapower Alignment**: How do land-based Eurasian economic integration initiatives (Heartland Theory) counter maritime blue-water containment strategies (Sea Power Theory) (Mackinder 1904; Mahan 1890)?
5. **Post-Hegemonic Order Scenarios**: What institutional architectures emerge when a declining hegemon is no longer capable of providing global public goods and the rising challenger is unwilling or unable to assume global leadership (Kindleberger 1973; Keohane 1984)?

## 3. Empirical Research Methodologies

The project employs a multi-method analytical workflow integrating historical narrative synthesis with econometric metric tracking:

### 3.1 Quantitative Time-Series Validation
Cross-referencing historical economic data (commodity price indices, interest rate spreads, sovereign debt-to-GDP ratios) across the three primary modern imperial transitions (Dutch Republic 1680–1780, British Empire 1815–1945, and United States 1945–Present) (Dalio 2021; Kennedy 1987).

### 3.2 Historiographical Comparative Analysis
Evaluating primary historical ledgers (Bank of Amsterdam balance sheets, Bank of England bullion reserves, US Federal Reserve H.4.1 releases) against rival theoretical models to eliminate confirmation bias and account for structural anomalies (Braudel 1984; Wallerstein 1974).

### 3.3 Counter-Factual Scenario Stress-Testing
Simulating systemic geopolitical shocks (e.g., weaponization of SWIFT, energy settlement de-dollarization, semiconductor export embargoes) against quantitative economic volatility bounds (Allison 2017).
