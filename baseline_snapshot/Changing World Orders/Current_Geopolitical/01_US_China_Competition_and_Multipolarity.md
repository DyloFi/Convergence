# US-China Strategic Competition, Hegemonic Rivalry, and the Multipolar Transition

## Executive Summary
The bilateral contest between the United States and the People's Republic of China constitutes the primary structural axis of contemporary international relations. As theorized by Graham Allison in *Destined for War* (2017) and A.F.K. Organski in *World Politics* (1958), this dynamic represents a classical Power Transition and Thucydides Trap dilemma: an incumbent hegemonic power, burdened by escalating sovereign debt and global security commitments, confronting a rapidly ascending challenger that possesses the industrial scale, technological sophistication, and geopolitical ambition to reshape the global order. Ray Dalio's *Principles for Dealing with the Changing World Order* (2021) conceptualizes this competition across five distinct, interlocking arenas of conflict: trade, technology, capital/currency, geopolitics/territory, and military/kinetic. This monograph provides an exhaustive empirical and theoretical analysis of each conflict domain, tracking the quantitative shift in global reserve currency shares, semiconductor supply chain bifurcation, naval balance in the Western Pacific, and the systemic risks of catastrophic great-power confrontation.

## Theoretical Foundations of Hegemonic Transition and Structural Rivalry

The structural tension between Washington and Beijing is grounded in three foundational international relations frameworks:

1. **Power Transition Theory (Organski & Kugler, 1980)**: Systemic war is most probable when a dissatisfied rising power approaches parity with the dominant status-quo hegemon (typically when the challenger reaches 80% to 120% of the hegemon's gross national capabilities). China reached approximately 115% of US GDP in purchasing power parity (PPP) terms in 2017, and currently stands at approximately 130% PPP, crossing the classic structural danger zone.
2. **The Thucydides Trap (Allison, 2017)**: Across 16 historical case studies of an emerging power threatening to displace an established ruling hegemon over the past 500 years, 12 culminated in catastrophic kinetic war. Escaping this dynamic requires unprecedented diplomatic accommodation and bilateral institutional guardrails.
3. **Offensive Realism (Mearsheimer, 2001)**: In an anarchic international system where state intentions are inherently uncertain and security cannot be guaranteed, great powers inevitably seek regional hegemony. Consequently, China will naturally attempt to dominate East Asia and push the United States out of the Western Pacific, while the United States will consistently seek to prevent Beijing from achieving regional hegemony.

## The Five Strategic Warfronts: Granular Empirical Audit

The bilateral competition operates across five asymmetric battlegrounds, each governed by distinct operational mechanisms and escalation thresholds.

### 1. The Trade and Industrial Warfront
Initiated with the US Section 301 tariffs under the Trump administration in 2018 and expanded under the Biden administration, the trade confrontation has evolved from simple tariff barriers into comprehensive industrial de-risking:
- **Tariff Coverage**: Over $350 billion of Chinese manufactured goods remain subject to Section 301 tariffs ranging from 7.5% to 25%, with targeted tariffs escalating up to 100% on Chinese electric vehicles, 50% on solar cells, and 25% on advanced steel and aluminum.
- **Supply Chain Diversification and "China+1"**: Western multinational corporations have rerouted final assembly operations to Vietnam, Mexico, and India. However, granular trade flow analysis reveals that Mexican and Vietnamese exports remain deeply reliant on intermediate component inputs and capital goods manufactured in mainland China, creating indirect rerouting rather than true structural decoupling.
- **Industrial Subsidies**: The passage of the US CHIPS and Science Act ($52.7 billion in direct subsidies) and the Inflation Reduction Act ($369 billion in energy transition incentives) represents an explicit Western adoption of state-directed industrial policy designed to counteract Beijing's *Made in China 2025* state-capitalist subsidies.

### 2. The High-Technology and Semiconductor Warfront
Semiconductors represent the foundational substrate of 21st-century economic and military power. The US Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) export controls of October 7, 2022, and subsequent expansions in October 2023 and 2024, established an aggressive technological containment strategy:
- **Extreme Ultraviolet (EUV) and Deep Ultraviolet (DUV) Embargoes**: Coordinated trilateral export restrictions between the United States, the Netherlands (ASML), and Japan (Tokyo Electron) restrict China's access to sub-14nm logic manufacturing equipment, sub-18nm DRAM equipment, and 128-layer NAND manufacturing tools.
- **Advanced Compute Accelerator Restrictions**: Bans on the export of advanced AI accelerators (NVIDIA A100, H100, H800, B200) to Chinese entities, designed to throttle Beijing's training of frontier foundation models.
- **Chinese Indigenous Breakthroughs**: In response, Chinese state champion Huawei and domestic foundry SMIC achieved commercial production of 7nm Kirin system-on-chips using multi-patterning immersion DUV lithography, while deploying Huawei Ascend 910B/910C AI accelerators to domestic tech giants (Alibaba, Tencent, Baidu), signaling the acceleration of a fully sovereign, non-Western compute stack.

### 4. The Geopolitical and Alliance Warfront
Geopolitical competition is manifested through opposing network architectures:
- **US Hub-and-Spoke Alliance Architecture**: Washington has revitalized regional security pacts across the Indo-Pacific, establishing the AUKUS trilateral submarine partnership with the UK and Australia, institutionalizing the Quad (US, Japan, India, Australia), securing expanded basing access in the Philippines under the Enhanced Defense Cooperation Agreement (EDCA), and solidifying trilateral Camp David coordination with Japan and South Korea.
- **Chinese Eurasian and Global South Alignment**: Beijing counters Western containment through the expansion of the Belt and Road Initiative (BRI), cementing the "no-limits" comprehensive strategic partnership with the Russian Federation, facilitating the historic diplomatic rapprochement between Saudi Arabia and Iran, and institutionalizing the expanded BRICS+ framework as an economic counterweight to the G7.

### 5. The Military, Deterrence, and Kinetic Warfront
The military balance in the Western Pacific has undergone a profound shift, severely challenging traditional American conventional deterrence:
- **Naval Hull Asymmetry**: The PLA Navy has expanded to over 370 operational battle-force ships, surpassing the US Navy's fleet of approximately 295 deployable vessels. While the US retains superior aggregate tonnage and nuclear-powered submarine advantages, China concentrates its naval assets exclusively within its home theater in the Western Pacific.
- **Anti-Access/Area-Denial (A2/AD) Missile Envelopes**: China's Rocket Force deploys hundreds of road-mobile DF-21D and hypersonic DF-26 "carrier killer" anti-ship ballistic missiles capable of striking US carrier strike groups out to 4,000 kilometers, placing US naval bases in Guam and Japan within high-density strike envelopes.
- **Taiwan Strait Flashpoint**: The primary trigger for potential kinetic confrontation remains Taiwan. China conducts continuous gray-zone air incursions across the median line and joint amphibious encirclement exercises (Joint Sword operations), while the US accelerates arms deliveries, Harpoon coastal defense missile deployments, and asymmetric "Hellscape" drone denial doctrines.

## Taiwan Strait Operational Dynamics and Wargame Kill-Chain Analysis

The central flashpoint of the bilateral rivalry is the status of Taiwan. Over the past five years, defense think tanks (Center for Strategic and International Studies [CSIS], RAND Corporation, Center for a New American Security [CNAS]) have conducted extensive iterative operational wargames examining potential kinetic scenarios:

### 1. CSIS "The First Battle of the Next War" Wargame Synthesis
Across 24 iterations of a 2026 Chinese amphibious invasion scenario, key empirical findings emerged:
- **US and Allied Munitions Depletion**: The United States expends its entire inventory of Long-Range Anti-Ship Missiles (LRASMs) and Joint Air-to-Surface Standoff Missiles (JASSM-ER) within the first 7 to 10 days of combat operations.
- **US Naval Attrition**: In typical base-case outcomes, the United States Navy loses two nuclear-powered aircraft carriers and between 10 to 20 large surface combatants (destroyers and cruisers) to land-based Chinese hypersonic anti-ship missile strikes and submarine torpedo attacks within the First Island Chain.
- **Chinese Amphibious Losses**: The PLA amphibious assault fleet suffers catastrophic attrition, losing over 100 amphibious vessels and tens of thousands of assault troops to Allied submarine torpedoes and standoff anti-ship missile strikes, leading in most scenarios to an invasion failure at enormous economic and material cost to all participants.
- **Taiwan Economic Destruction**: Taiwan's domestic electricity grid, transportation infrastructure, and advanced semiconductor fabrication facilities (TSMC Fab 18, Fab 20) are largely incapacitated within the first 72 hours, precipitating an instantaneous global economic depression with estimated worldwide GDP losses exceeding $10 trillion in the first year alone.

### 2. Gray-Zone Coercion and Quarantine Scenarios
Rather than a high-risk amphibious assault, military analysts increasingly project that Beijing may execute a maritime customs quarantine or partial naval blockade:
- **Quarantine Mechanics**: The China Coast Guard (CCG) and Maritime Militia declare an environmental or customs inspection zone around Taiwan, intercepting commercial container vessels and liquefied natural gas (LNG) tankers.
- **Escalation Asymmetry**: This places the burden of kinetic escalation entirely on the United States and Taiwan: Washington must choose between initiating offensive strikes against civilian-flagged coast guard cutters or acquiescing to Beijing's de facto administrative jurisdiction over Taiwan's maritime approaches.
- **Energy Fragility**: Taiwan maintains only 8 to 11 days of domestic natural gas reserves, rendering its economy acutely vulnerable to commercial shipping disruptions without a single missile being launched.

## Capital Account Dynamics: The Great Sovereign Re-Anchoring

A decisive structural vector of the financial confrontation is the multi-year liquidation of US sovereign debt holdings by the Chinese state:
- **Treasury Divestment**: China's official holdings of US Treasury securities peaked at $1.32 trillion in November 2013. By early 2026, official holdings declined to approximately $730 billion—a structural reduction of over 44%.
- **Shadow Reserve Diversification**: Cliometric analysis by Brad Setser and the Council on Foreign Relations indicates that rather than repatriating these funds to domestic onshore accounts, Chinese state entities have redirected capital into agency debt, European sovereign bonds, offshore private equity, and physical gold accumulation.
- **Gold Accumulation Trajectory**: The People's Bank of China has reported official gold reserve increases for over 18 consecutive months, expanding official holdings to over 2,260 metric tons, with independent bullion analysts estimating actual state-controlled gold reserves exceeding 5,000 metric tons when accounting for the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) and domestic state-owned commercial bank inventories.

## Macroeconomic Imbalances and Capital Account Paradoxes: The Pettis-Triffin Dynamic

Beyond military hardware and microchips, the deepest structural engine of US-China friction resides in macroeconomic accounting identities, as rigorously detailed by Michael Pettis in *Trade Wars Are Class Wars* (2020):

### 1. Structural Underconsumption and Chinese Industrial Subsidies
China's economic development model features an extraordinarily low share of domestic household consumption relative to GDP (approximately 38%, compared to a global average of 60% and a US figure of 68%):
- **Mechanism of Repressed Consumption**: State policies historically repressed household income through suppressed deposit interest rates, controlled rural land rights, and weak social safety nets, transferring economic surplus into state-directed industrial investment and infrastructure.
- **The Inevitability of Trade Surpluses**: Because domestic Chinese households do not earn enough income to consume the vast physical output produced by China's factories, the Chinese economy structurally produces more than it consumes ($Savings > Investment$). By definition, this structural excess output must be absorbed by the rest of the world as a massive trade surplus, which approached $1 trillion annually by 2024–2025.

### 2. The Exorbitant Burden of the US Capital Account Surplus
Under the classic Triffin Dilemma and floating fiat exchange rates, the United States, as the provider of the world's primary reserve asset, cannot unilaterally eliminate its trade deficit without restricting foreign access to dollar liquidity:
- **Foreign Capital Recycling**: Because China, Japan, and surplus emerging markets run persistent current account surpluses, they must purchase foreign assets to balance their international payments. Due to the unparalleled depth, liquidity, and open legal protections of US financial markets, this foreign capital flows overwhelmingly into US Treasuries, corporate equities, and real estate.
- **Forced Deficits on the Hegemon**: As Pettis demonstrates, foreign capital inflows force the United States to run a matching capital account surplus, which mathematically requires the United States to run a current account deficit ($Current Account + Capital Account = 0$). To absorb this capital, the US economy must either decrease domestic savings (by expanding federal budget deficits or inflating household debt) or accept higher domestic unemployment and industrial factory closures.
- **The Political Backlash**: This macroeconomic transmission mechanism systematically hollows out domestic American manufacturing employment in the Rust Belt while enriching the financial sector on Wall Street. The resulting domestic economic inequality and political polarization fuel protectionist trade tariffs and nationalist populism, transforming an accounting identity into acute geopolitical conflict.

## Cross-Cycle Convergence and Master Synthesis Coupling

The US-China strategic competition represents the central crucible in which the broader macro-cycles converge:
- **Dalio's Big Cycle**: Marks the textbook transition from Phase 5 to Phase 6. The incumbent hegemon suffers from extreme fiscal indebtedness, unmanageable interest obligations on sovereign debt, and profound internal political polarization, while the rising power challenges the international status quo across trade, currency, and borders.
- **Kondratiev Long Wave (K-Wave 6)**: The battle for AI supremacy, quantum computing, and advanced materials between Washington and Beijing constitutes the state-sponsored industrial engine of the emerging Sixth Kondratiev Wave. Hegemonic power in the 21st century will accrue to the power that achieves unilateral leadership in AI-driven industrial productivity and automated robotics.
- **Strauss & Howe Fourth Turning**: The escalating risk of external war is directly amplified by the Fourth Turning Crisis dynamic. Domestic institutional paralysis within the United States limits diplomatic flexibility, while economic pressures inside China (property debt deflation, aging demographics) create strong incentives for nationalist foreign policy mobilization.

## Strategic Policy Recommendations: Guardrails and De-Escalation Architecture

To prevent structural rivalry from degenerating into catastrophic kinetic confrontation, historical cliometrics and game-theoretic modeling suggest three mandatory bilateral policy initiatives:
- **Military Crisis Communication Hotlines and AI Guardrails**: Re-establishing direct, uninhibited flag-officer communications between US INDOPACOM and the PLA Eastern/Southern Theater Commands, while negotiating bilateral treaties restricting autonomous AI control over nuclear command, control, and communication (NC3) systems.
- **Calibrated Commercial De-Risking vs Total Autarky**: Confining high-technology export restrictions strictly to narrow "small yard, high fence" national security technologies (advanced AI hardware and quantum encryption) while maintaining open, reciprocal bilateral trade in non-sensitive commercial sectors (consumer electronics, pharmaceuticals, agricultural commodities).
- **Mutual Maritime Code for Unplanned Encounters at Sea (CUES)**: Expanding the CUES protocol to explicitly govern unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), autonomous subsurface vessels (UUVs), and maritime militia coast guard cutters operating in contested littoral zones.
