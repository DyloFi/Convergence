## 3. Comparative Perspectives, Critiques & Counter-Theories (Media Analysis) (Media Analysis Deep Dive)

Analyzing media sentiment in long-wave economic theory faces significant econometric skepticism:

1. **Efficient Market Hypothesis & Narrative Noise (Fama, 1970; Solow, 1956)**: Mainstream finance theorists argue that media sentiment represents superficial noise. Asset prices reflect fundamental discounted cash flows rather than journalistic narratives.
2. **Spectral Analysis & Filter Artifacts (Solomou, 1987)**: Econometricians show that media sentiment cycles cannot prove 50-year periodicity. Moving-average filters applied to sentiment series artificially induce smooth 50-year oscillations (the Slutsky-Yule Effect).
3. **Stochastic Unit Roots in Sentiment (Nelson & Plosser, 1982)**: Media sentiment indices follow non-stationary stochastic random walks $I(1)$:
$$S_t = \mu + S_{t-1} + \epsilon_t$$
   Unpredicted exogenous events (wars, policy shocks, breakthroughs) drive sentiment shifts rather than predictable long-wave cycles.
