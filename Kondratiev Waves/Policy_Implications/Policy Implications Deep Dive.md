# State Industrial Policy & Institutional Adaptation in Kondratiev Long Waves

## Executive Summary — Policy Implications
This research report provides a comprehensive analysis of **State Industrial Policy and Institutional Governance** across Kondratiev long waves. Grounded in the socio-technical paradigm frameworks of Carlota Perez (2002), Christopher Freeman & Francisco Louçã (2001), and Mariana Mazzucato (2013), this study examines how state intervention, strategic R&D funding, infrastructure investment, and regulatory restructuring evolve across K-Wave phases—focusing on contemporary 6th Wave industrial policies (e.g., US CHIPS and Science Act, Inflation Reduction Act, EU AI Act).

## 1. Theoretical Framework & Core Principles (Policy Implications)

State policy plays a decisive, non-neutral role in enabling or delaying long-wave technological transitions across three core dimensions:

### 1.1 Co-Evolution of Techno-Economic and Institutional Subsystems
Freeman and Louçã (2001) demonstrate that long waves require structural alignment across five co-evolving subsystems: technology, economy, culture, science, and public policy. When institutional frameworks lag behind radical technological shifts (institutional inertia), economic friction and structural stagnation occur until state policy undergoes fundamental recalibration.

### 1.2 Perez State Regulation across Paradigm Phases
Carlota Perez (2002) outlines the shifting role of government across paradigm lifecycle phases:
- **Installation Phase (Laissez-Faire & Deregulation) (State Industrial Policy & Institutional Adaptation in Kondratiev Long Waves)**: Financial capital dominates; state policy favors deregulation, low capital gains taxes, and speculative market expansion.
- **Turning Point Crisis (Re-Regulation & State Intervention) (State Industrial Policy & Institutional Adaptation in Kondratiev Long Waves)**: Bursting asset bubbles force state intervention, emergency bailouts, and structural re-regulation (e.g., British Joint Stock Companies Act 1844, US Glass-Steagall Act 1933, Dodd-Frank Act 2010).
- **Deployment Phase (State-Guided Golden Age) (State Industrial Policy & Institutional Adaptation in Kondratiev Long Waves)**: Production capital dominates; state industrial policy directs infrastructure expansion, public R&D, and demand stabilization (e.g., New Deal, Marshall Plan, Post-WWII Keynesian Welfare State).

### 1.3 The Entrepreneurial State (Mazzucato Framework)
Mariana Mazzucato (2013) refutes the view that state policy merely corrects market failures, demonstrating that public sector investments fund the highest-risk early R&D that creates General Purpose Technologies (e.g., DARPA internet investments, NIH bio-tech research, US federal GPU clusters).

## 3. Comparative Perspectives, Critiques & Counter-Theories (Policy Implications) (Policy Implications Deep Dive)

State industrial policy in long-wave economics faces substantial academic and political skepticism:

1. **Neoclassical Efficiency & Public Choice Critiques (Solow, 1956; Buchanan, 1986)**: Neoclassical growth theorists and Public Choice economists argue that state industrial policy distorts market price signals, encourages rent-seeking, and results in inefficient capital allocation compared to free market competition.
2. **Spectral Analysis & Filter Artifacts (Solomou, 1987)**: Solomou demonstrates that attributing long-term growth shifts to specific state policies is methodologically flawed, as moving-average filters create artificial periodicities (the Slutsky-Yule Effect).
3. **Unit Roots & Exogenous Policy Shocks (Nelson & Plosser, 1982)**: Macroeconomic time series possess unit roots $I(1)$, showing that major policy interventions (e.g., 1933 New Deal or 2022 CHIPS Act) act as permanent stochastic structural breaks rather than components of a deterministic 50-year wave.
4. **Marxist Theory of the Capitalist State (Mandel, 1975)**: Ernest Mandel argued that state industrial policy cannot eliminate long-wave contraction phases because public intervention ultimately serves to protect capital profitability $\frac{s}{c+v}$ rather than resolve underlying structural contradictions.
