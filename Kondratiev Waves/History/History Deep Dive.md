# Kondratiev Waves: How Well Do the Standard Datings Agree?

*Rewritten from the evidence up in Step 13 (2026-10-17). The previous version, a generic history with an unsourced "longitudinal matrix" for 1780-2026, is preserved at `archive/step13_originals/Kondratiev Waves/History/History Deep Dive.md.orig`.*

## Executive Summary

The pillar describes "five completed Kondratiev waves" and a sixth that began around 2020. Laying the main published datings side by side shows how much that depends on whose dates are used. Kondratiev himself dated only two and a half waves: 1790-1849 (turning point 1815), 1850-1896 (turning point 1873), and a new upswing from 1896. Carlota Perez dates five technological revolutions from "big bang" events in 1771, 1829, 1875, 1908 and 1971, and treats the ICT surge that began in 1971 as still unfolding. Daniel Šmihula dates six waves of shrinking length, starting in 1600, 1780, 1880, 1940, 1985 and 2015. Leo Nefiodow puts the start of a sixth wave in 2000-2003. The start of the "current" wave therefore ranges from 1971 to 2020 depending on the author, a spread of nearly 50 years, about one full wave length. The broad sequence of leading technologies (textile machinery, steam and railways, steel and electricity, oil and cars, ICT) is common ground. The dates and the count of waves are not. The pillar should present the waves as overlapping technological eras with contested boundaries, not as five fixed historical periods.

## Question and scope

**Question.** How closely do the main published datings of Kondratiev waves agree on the number of waves and their start dates, and what does that disagreement mean for using the waves as a historical frame?

**Scope.** Four datings: Kondratiev (1920s-1935), Perez (2002, as tabulated in Perez 2009), Šmihula and Nefiodow (on the sixth wave). The timing of individual crises is covered in `Case_Studies/Case Studies Deep Dive.md` and the statistical evidence in `Economics/Economics Deep Dive.md`. Not covered: commodity price series. The repository holds none (see `Data/README.md`), and the earlier version's price table had no sources.

## Methodology

For each author I take the published start dates and labels. I then compare (1) the number of waves since the late 18th century, (2) the start dates of comparable waves, and (3) where each author places the present. Dates come from Perez's own table and, for Kondratiev, Šmihula and Nefiodow, from the summary in the Wikipedia article on Kondratiev waves, which cites their works.

## Empirical Evidence

### Side-by-side datings

| Leading technology (common label) | Kondratiev | Perez (big bang) | Šmihula |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Mechanised textiles / Industrial Revolution | 1790-1849 (turning point 1815) | 1771 (Arkwright's mill, Cromford) | 1780-1880 (one wave covering both) |
| Steam and railways | 1850-1896 (turning point 1873) | 1829 (*Rocket* trial, Liverpool-Manchester railway) | (included above) |
| Steel, electricity, heavy engineering | new wave from 1896 | 1875 (Carnegie Bessemer plant, Pittsburgh) | 1880-1940 |
| Oil, automobile, mass production | — | 1908 (first Model T, Detroit) | 1940-1985 |
| Information and telecommunications | — | 1971 (Intel microprocessor announced) | 1985-2015 |
| "Sixth wave" | — | not dated | 2015-2035? (hypothetical) |

Nefiodow places the end of the fifth wave in the 2000-2003 crisis and the start of a sixth at the same time, carried by health and biotechnology. Other authors expect a sixth wave led by resource efficiency and clean technology.

### What the comparison shows

- **Number of waves.** By 1971, Perez counts five revolutions since 1771. Šmihula counts four waves since 1600 by 1940 and puts the textile and railway eras into one.
- **Start dates.** For the steel and electricity era, starts range from 1875 (Perez) to 1896 (Kondratiev). For mass production, they range from 1908 (Perez) to 1940 (Šmihula). That is a 32-year gap, more than half a wave.
- **Where we are now.** The current wave began in 1971 (Perez, still in the ICT surge), 1985 (Šmihula's fifth), around 2000-2003 (Nefiodow's sixth) or around 2015 (Šmihula's sixth). This repository has used 2020. The spread is about 49 years.
- **Agreement.** All the datings agree on the *order* of leading technologies and on Britain's early lead, with later leadership shifting to the USA and Germany (Perez's table names the core countries for each revolution).

## Competing views and counterarguments

- **Defence of the datings.** Perez argues that revolutions overlap: the deployment of one coexists with the irruption of the next, so a single start date is a simplification. On this view, the disagreement reflects different choices of marker (first innovation, peak investment, price turning point), not a failure of the concept.
- **Critique.** If each author picks a different marker and the resulting dates differ by up to half a wave, then saying "we are in phase X of wave six" carries no information until the marker is fixed and tested. A cycle whose phase cannot be agreed on cannot be used for timing.
- **Different mechanisms.** Kondratiev dated waves from prices and interest rates. Perez and Šmihula date them from technologies. These are different phenomena that may not move together, especially after 1945, when price waves vanish (see `Economics/Economics Deep Dive.md`).

## Limitations

- Kondratiev's, Šmihula's and Nefiodow's dates are taken from a secondary summary (Wikipedia), not from the original works. Perez's dates are from her own working paper.
- Only four datings are compared. Others (Schumpeter 1939, Freeman and Louçã 2001, Modelski and Thompson 1996) would widen the range, not narrow it.
- The comparison is about dates and counts. It does not test whether the waves exist (see the Economics report).

## Conclusion

The leading-technology sequence behind Kondratiev waves is well established as economic history. The dates are not. Published datings disagree on how many waves there have been and on when the current one began, by up to about 49 years. The Convergence synthesis should stop saying "the sixth Kondratiev wave began in 2020" as if it were a fact. It should say that several authors propose a new technological wave (candidates are AI, biotechnology and clean energy), that its start date is contested, and which indicators would show its deployment.
