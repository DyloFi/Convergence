1. **Emergence / R&D Phase (Technology Paradigms & Diffusion Dynamics in Kondratiev Long Waves)**: High technical uncertainty, low efficiency, slow adoption.
2. **Accelerated Diffusion (A-Phase Expansion) (Technology Paradigms & Diffusion Dynamics in Kondratiev Long Waves)**: Rapid cost decline, standardized architecture, massive capital inflows, industrial swarming.
3. **Maturity / Saturation (B-Phase Contraction) (Technology Paradigms & Diffusion Dynamics in Kondratiev Long Waves)**: Exhaustion of physical efficiency gains, diminishing returns on R&D, market saturation.

### 1.2 Mensch's Depression-Trigger Hypothesis
Gerhard Mensch (1979) established that during economic expansions, established corporations focus on low-risk **improvement innovations**. Only when an economy enters a severe depression ("technological stalemate") do investors take the risk of funding **basic radical innovations**, causing basic innovations to cluster during K-wave Winter troughs.

### 1.3 Perez Socio-Technical Paradigm Transformations
Carlota Perez (2002) demonstrates that technological revolutions are not merely hardware upgrades, but comprehensive **techno-economic paradigm shifts** that re-engineer organizational models, skill requirements, infrastructure networks, and institutional regulations.

## 3. Comparative Perspectives, Critiques & Counter-Theories (Technology) (Technology Deep Dive)

Technological determinism in long-wave economic theory faces significant academic critiques:

1. **The Solow Productivity Paradox (Solow, 1987; Gordon, 2016)**: Robert Solow and Robert Gordon argue that modern digital and AI technologies, while impressive, generate less aggregate total factor productivity (TFP) growth than earlier 2nd and 3rd wave innovations (indoor plumbing, electricity, internal combustion engine).
2. **Spectral Analysis & Filter Artifacts (Solomou, 1987)**: Econometricians show that trend-elimination filters applied to patent data or technology output series artificially project 50-year periodicities (the Slutsky-Yule Effect).
3. **Unit Roots & Stochastic Innovation (Nelson & Plosser, 1982)**: Technological innovation follows non-stationary stochastic random walks $I(1)$, proving that technological breakthroughs occur as unpredicted random events rather than deterministic 50-year cycles.
4. **Neoclassical Smooth Exogenous Growth (Solow, 1956)**: Standard growth theory treats technological progress as a smooth continuous exogenous parameter $A(t) = A_0 e^{gt}$.
